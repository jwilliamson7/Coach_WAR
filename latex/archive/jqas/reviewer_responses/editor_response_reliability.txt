I believe there was miscommunication. Although section 4.4 is titled “WAR Persistence and Scheme Obsolescence”, my intent behind this section was to note the explicit lack of evidence for persistence. I agree with the reviewer that a single-season autocorrelation this low is not, on its own, evidence of a persistent coaching ability, and I have rewritten the opening of the section so that this is explicit. The substantive question is the right one, namely, how to distinguish a real but noisy coaching signal from pure statistical noise, and I address it directly below.
First, most of the unexplained single-season variance is irreducible sampling noise. A 16-game season carries a binomial sampling floor of about 1.94 wins even for a model that knew each team's true win probability exactly, and the model's test RMSE of about 2.5 wins sits only modestly above that floor. A single season is therefore an inherently noisy measurement of coaching quality, in the same way that a single season of BABIP is a noisy measurement of pitching.
Second, the data contains a pattern that pure noise would not be expected to produce. If Coaching WAR were only noise, I would expect offensive- and defensive-background coaches to show the same persistence. Instead, defensive coaches show more multi-year persistence than offensive coaches (two-year-average R^2 of 0.18 versus 0.05; Fisher's Z test p = 0.001, Appendix E).
“Scheme obsolescence" is offered only as a speculative mechanism for why the genuine persistent component decays over time, consistent with the faster decay of offensive schemes; I have softened the wording of the section so that this is clear (in line with the other reviewer’s feedback).
